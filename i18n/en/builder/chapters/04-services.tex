\chapter{Services}
\label{ch:builder-services}

\section{What will you build in this chapter?}
\label{sec:builder-services-intro}

A service is how one or more forms reach applicants on the organization's portal, under a clear name, with a guide they read before they start, conditions that decide who may apply, fees and a known path. This chapter explains how to create a service from the “Services” page, then set up its page tabs one by one: Profile, Delivery \& eligibility, Path \& forms and Fees. It then covers publishing it on the portal, receiving walk-in requests at service centers, and following how requests perform and where they wait.

Service settings apply on the portal as soon as you save them, without republishing any form, while changes to the form itself apply only after you publish it in the form builder. So build and publish the form first, then create the service around it.

\begin{rcnote}[Example: Horizon]
Horizon wants its customers to request a branch visit on the portal, so the system administrator creates a “Branch visit request” service around the published form of the same name and makes the visit an in-person step at the Aleppo Branch. Omar Haddad then registers walk-in requests at the intake desk, and Sarah Al-Khatib follows the service's performance and its late requests.
\end{rcnote}

\section{Pages in this chapter}
\label{sec:builder-services-pages}

You start from the “Services” page in Builder's “Core” section, and from there open each service on a page with five tabs. The pages are listed in the suggested build order: follow it the first time, then return to any page when you need it.

\thh{Page and section} & \thh{What this page offers}\\
Services — Core & Lists the services on the portal and the internal forms; from here you create a new service and choose how it is delivered.\\
Service overview — Service page & Sums up the service's readiness, its last 30 days, late requests and change history.\\
