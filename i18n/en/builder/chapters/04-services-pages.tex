\section{Services}
\label{sec:builder-feature-services}

A service is what applicants see on the organization's portal, such as “Certificate request” or “Book an appointment”. Each service holds one or more forms and decides how it is delivered and who may apply. So a form reaches applicants only through a service; a form that belongs to no service stays an internal form that only staff use. The “Services” page gathers every service in one table, and from it you create a new service and open the intake desk and service performance.

\textbf{Path and who uses it:} “Builder” → “Core” → “Services”. Used by whoever manages the organization's public services, when creating a service or reviewing the state of existing ones.

\begin{rcnote}[Example: Horizon]
Horizon's system administrator presses “New service” ①, names the service “Branch visit request” and chooses “Hybrid”, because the request starts on the portal and the visit itself happens at the Aleppo Branch. They then choose the published “Branch visit request” form, while Horizon's other forms stay under “Internal forms” ④.
\end{rcnote}

\section{Service overview}
\label{sec:builder-feature-services-overview}

Every service opens on the “Overview” tab, a summary that answers three questions: is the service ready, how is it doing, and what needs you now. It starts with the “Service readiness” list, which checks each part of the setup, then the last 30 days' numbers, then late requests. So return to it each time you open the service to know where to start.

\textbf{Path and who uses it:} “Services” → press the service → “Overview”. Used by whoever builds the service to finish its setup, and by whoever follows it to see which requests are late.

\begin{rcnote}[Example: Horizon]
The system administrator opens the “Branch visit request” service and sees in “Service readiness” ① that “Fees” are not set up and “Center intake” is off. They press “Turn on” next to center intake to go straight to the “Delivery \& eligibility” tab.

\section{Profile and service guide}
\label{sec:builder-feature-services-profile}

The “Profile” tab is what applicants read before they start: the service name and description, and its guide with processing time, fees and required documents. The clearer the guide, the fewer incomplete requests and repeated questions. The tab also sets the service's portal address and where it appears among apps and sections.

\textbf{Path and who uses it:} “Services” → press the service → “Profile”. Filled in by whoever builds the service, and updated by whoever answers applicants' questions when a procedure changes.

\begin{rcnote}[Example: Horizon]
The system administrator writes “3 working days” in “Processing time” and uses “Add a document” ③ to add a copy of the ID as a required document. They then choose the “Branch services” app in “Place on the portal” ④, so customers find the service there.

\section{Delivery and eligibility}
\label{sec:builder-feature-services-delivery}

The “Delivery \& eligibility” tab sets how the service is delivered, when it is available, and who may apply on what conditions. It stops requests that do not qualify before they start, instead of rejecting them after they arrive. The sections shown depend on the delivery mode: a service starting on the portal shows the eligibility and verification sections, and a service with in-person steps shows service centers, appointments and intake.

\textbf{Path and who uses it:} “Services” → press the service → “Delivery \& eligibility”. Set by whoever builds the service, who comes back to pause it or change its conditions.

\begin{rcnote}[Example: Horizon]
For the “Branch visit request” service, the system administrator chooses “Registered accounts only” ③ and sets “Open requests per applicant” to one, so a customer does not repeat the same request. They then choose the Aleppo Branch under service centers and turn on “Center intake”.

\section{Service centers}
\label{sec:builder-feature-services-centers}

Service centers are where applicants go for in-person steps, such as handing in originals or collecting the result. Centers are defined once for the whole organization, then each service chooses the ones it needs, and they show on the portal with the address and opening hours. Each center also sets the staff whose intake desk opens on it.

\textbf{Path and who uses it:} “Services” → press a service whose mode is not “Fully online” → “Delivery \& eligibility” → “Manage centers”. Set by the services administrator when a center opens or its hours or staff change.

\begin{rcnote}[Example: Horizon]
