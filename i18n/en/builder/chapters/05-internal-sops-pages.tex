\section{Internal SOPs}
\label{sec:builder-feature-internal-sops}

The “Internal SOPs” page is the starting point for every SOP you build. It lists existing SOPs in folders, shows each SOP's state and its recent run results, and lets you create a new SOP or open an existing one to edit it. Its health signals help you notice an SOP that has started failing before anyone complains.

\textbf{Path and who uses it:} “Builder” → “Core” → “Internal SOPs”. Used by those who design the organization's workflows and follow how they run.

\begin{rcnote}[Example: Horizon]
The system administrator builds an “Approve visit request” SOP that starts “On Form Submitted” for the visit request form, sends an “Approval Request” to Sarah Al-Khatib, then creates a task for Omar Haddad to prepare the visit if she approves. The SOP starts from ② and its runs are followed in ④.
\end{rcnote}

\subsection{Creating a new SOP}

“New SOP” opens a side sheet that asks for two things only: the name, and the answer to “What starts it?”. The sheet shows the most used triggers, “On Form Submitted”, “New Record Created”, “Schedule”, “On Approval Finished”, “Generic Webhook” and “Manual”; the others are behind “More triggers”. You can change both the name and the trigger later in the editor.

\begin{rcwarn}[Deleting a folder that holds SOPs]
If you delete a folder that contains SOPs or subfolders, you must choose what happens to its contents: move them to the top of the list, or archive them and remove the folder. An empty folder is deleted directly.
\end{rcwarn}

\item Press “New SOP”, or choose the create-SOP command from a folder's menu.
\item Type the name, choose a common trigger or one from “More triggers”, then press “Create and open”.
\item Come back to the list later and choose “Needs attention” to see live SOPs whose recent runs failed.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Check and recovery:} the editor should open on the new SOP, and the list should show it as “Draft” with the note “Not published yet”. If you cannot find an SOP you know, choose “All” and clear the search, then try “Archived”: an archived SOP does not appear under “All” until you “Restore” it.

\section{The SOP editor and its modes}
\label{sec:builder-feature-isop-editor}

The editor is where you draw the SOP step by step. It has a header showing the SOP's state, a “Steps” list beside the canvas, and the canvas where you see the steps and the paths between them. The editor has three modes: “Build” to edit steps, “Test” to try the draft, and “Runs” to see on the same canvas what happened in each run. Your edits save automatically to a draft.

\textbf{Path and who uses it:} “Internal SOPs” → press the SOP's name. Used by whoever builds or fixes the SOP.

\begin{rcnote}[Example: Horizon]
The administrator opens “Approve visit request” and sees in the “Steps” list that the “Approval Request” step carries the “Needs setup” badge. He presses it, finishes its setup, then writes a note (③) on the canvas explaining to colleagues why Sarah's approval is requested.

\section{Triggers: what starts the SOP?}
\label{sec:builder-feature-isop-triggers}

A trigger is the event that starts a new run of the SOP, and it is always the first step in the path. It may be a system event such as a record being created or a form being submitted, a recurring schedule, or a member pressing run. A trigger works only after the SOP is published, and an SOP can contain more than one trigger.

\textbf{Path and who uses it:} the “New SOP” sheet → “What starts it?” → “More triggers”, or “Add trigger” in the editor, or “Change trigger” in the trigger step's setup. Used by whoever builds the SOP.

\begin{rcnote}[Example: Horizon]
The administrator chooses “New Record Created” (④) and sets “Table” to the “Visit requests” record type. Each time Omar Haddad adds a new request such as \en{HZN-VR-0001}, a new run of the SOP starts.
\end{rcnote}

\section{Control-flow steps}
\label{sec:builder-feature-isop-control-flow}

Control-flow steps do no work in the system; they decide the SOP's route: does it branch? does it repeat steps for each item in a list? does it wait for a time or an event? when does it end? You find them all in the “Control flow” category when adding a step. Thanks to them, the SOP follows the rule the organization actually works by, not a single fixed path.

\textbf{Path and who uses it:} the editor → the add button → “Control flow”. Used by whoever builds the SOP.

\begin{rcnote}[Example: Horizon]
After “Approval Request”, the administrator adds a “Decision” (②) with two paths: the first continues to creating Omar's task when the approval outcome is approved, and the second ends with an “End” step when Sarah requests changes.
\end{rcnote}

\label{fig:builder-isop-control-flow-screen}
\shotnote{The image shows the step menu after pressing the add button, open on the “Control flow” category with its twelve steps and a short description of each. The descriptions help you pick the step that decides the SOP's route before you add it. ① “Control flow”: the category that groups control steps. ② “Decision”: routes the SOP into named paths. ③ “For Each”: repeats steps for each item in a list. ④ “Delay”: pauses the SOP for a duration or until a date.}
\end{figure}

\begin{table}[H]
\centering

\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{R{3.6cm} Y}
\rcHeaderRow
\thh{Step} & \thh{What it does}\\
Decision & Routes the SOP into named paths, each with a label and a condition. It starts with two paths and a fallback path when nothing matches; “Matching paths” chooses between “First matching path” and “All matching paths”.\\
For Each & Repeats the body steps for each item in “Items”, then continues after them. Inside the loop you can use the current item and its position.\\
Delay & Pauses the SOP for a “Duration” such as 1 day, or “Until date”.\\
Wait For & Waits until a matching internal event, record state or external callback arrives, with an optional timeout.\\

Call SOP & Runs a published SOP whose trigger is “Called by Another SOP” and waits for its response. The chosen version stays fixed for that call, even on retry.\\
Return SOP Response & Returns a value to the SOP that called this one.\\
Run JavaScript & Runs short code in an isolated sandbox without network, and returns a value the next steps can use.\\
\end{tabularx}
\end{table}

Only one connection can leave an ordinary step. If you need two paths, add a “Decision”; otherwise the issue “Only a Decision can split into several paths.” appears. Every path of a decision must also have a name.
\end{rcwarn}

\item Press “Add path” if you need another path, and choose the selection method in “Matching paths”.
\item Add the next step or an “End” step at the end of each path.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Check and recovery:} the canvas should show one line per path carrying its label, and the warning “The decision's conditions don't match its paths.” should disappear. If the test takes the wrong path, open the previous step's result in the test and check the value the condition reads.

\subsection{Steps and check}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Open “Requests for you” and choose the request from the “Needs you” tab.
\item Read the request's title, details and the attached record, if any.
\item Press “Approve” and sign, or press “Request changes” and write the comment.
\item As the SOP's builder, open the run in “Runs” and check that it continued after the response.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Check and recovery:} the request should leave “Needs you” and appear under “Completed”, and the run should continue after the response. If the approver cannot find the request, make sure they are in the approval stages and that the run actually reached the “Approval Request” step. If the response is late, send a reminder from the request.

\section{Review, publish and version history}
\label{sec:builder-feature-isop-publish}

An SOP runs only after it is published, and your edits do not reach the live version until you publish again. Before publishing, “Review \& publish” shows what must be fixed, the changes since the live version and the last test result. After publishing, the version is locked: runs in progress finish on their old version, and new runs use the new one.

\textbf{Path and who uses it:} the editor → “Review \& publish”; version history from the SOP name in the editor header. Used by whoever builds the SOP.

\begin{rcnote}[Example: Horizon]
The administrator adds a “Send Notification” step to the live SOP, and it appears under “Changes since v1 (live)” marked “Added”. He publishes v2, and a visit request already waiting for Sarah's approval on v1 stays on v1 until it ends.
\end{rcnote}

\subsection{Steps and check}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Press “Review \& publish” in the editor header.
\item Press “Fix” next to each issue, fix the step, then return to the review.
\item Review the changes since the live version, and run a test if the draft is untested.
\item Press “Publish vN”.
\item To review an earlier version, press the SOP name in the editor header and open “Version history”.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Check and recovery:} the message “vN is live” should appear, and the version badge should change to “Live · vN”. If “Nothing to publish: the draft matches the live version.” appears, nothing changed since the last publish. If “The draft changed during publication.” appears, publish again to include your latest edits.
