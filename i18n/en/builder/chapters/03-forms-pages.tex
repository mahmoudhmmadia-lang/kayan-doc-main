\section{Forms}
\label{sec:builder-feature-forms}

A form is a page of questions an employee or applicant fills in, such as a leave request or a visit form. The “Forms List” page shows all the organization's forms grouped by app and section; from it you create a new form or open an existing one in the “Form Builder”. People always work on the published version of a form, while your edits stay in a draft that does not reach them before you publish.

\textbf{Path and who uses it:} “Builder” → “Core” → “Forms”. Used by those with forms permission to create new forms, duplicate them or move them between organizations.

\begin{rcnote}[Example: Horizon]
The system administrator presses ① “Create Form”, writes “Branch visit request” as the form name, chooses the “Branch services” app and one of its sections, then writes a short reference prefix that marks this form's requests. Later he finds the form quickly by typing its name in ③ Search.
\end{rcnote}

\section{The form builder and its tools}
\label{sec:builder-feature-forms-builder}

The “Form Builder” is where you design the whole form. In its middle a canvas shows the form's pages and questions as the person filling it will see them, and beside it the “Form tools” rail opens each tool in its own panel. The builder header shows the save status, the button to try the form, and “Publish”.

\textbf{Path and who uses it:} “Forms” → press the form's name. Used by those who may edit the form; more than one person can work on the same draft.

\begin{rcnote}[Example: Horizon]
The system administrator opens the “Branch visit request” form and sees on the rail how many rules and record questions it has. He then opens “AI” and writes: “Add a question about the number of visitors after the branch question”, reviews the proposed change and presses “Apply”.
\end{rcnote}

\subsection{The form AI assistant}
Open “AI” and write what you want in the “Describe the form change you want” box, such as adding questions, changing checks or adding an action. The assistant does not change the form directly: it shows “AI draft changes” with how much was added, updated and removed, and you can open “Change details”. Then press “Apply” to carry them out or “Discard” to drop them. This button appears when AI in Builder is turned on for the organization.

\begin{rcwarn}[When you open an old version]
If you open an earlier version from “Versions”, most tools are disabled because it is read-only. To change it, press “Edit as draft”, which starts a new draft from it. If the message “This draft changed in another editor” appears, press “Reload latest draft” after copying any unsaved work.
\end{rcwarn}

\section{Pages, sections and tables}
\label{sec:builder-feature-forms-pages}

A long form is divided into pages, so the person filling it sees one part at a time and moves between them. Each page holds sections that group related questions, and a section can repeat to enter several sets of the same answers, such as several visitors. A table shows questions as columns and adds a row for each entry.

\textbf{Path and who uses it:} “Form Builder” → the pages bar above the canvas, and the add bar under each section. Used by the person designing the form.

\begin{rcnote}[Example: Horizon]
The system administrator splits “Branch visit request” into two pages: “Visit details” and “Visitors”. On the second page he places a table of visitor names and turns on “User can add rows”, so Omar Haddad adds one row per visitor.
\end{rcnote}

\section{Testing, publishing and versions}
\label{sec:builder-feature-forms-test-publish}

Before the form reaches people, you try it in an isolated test workspace as a member you choose, so you see what they see with their roles and permissions. You then publish it, and the draft becomes a new published version that people fill. “Versions” keeps every version, so you can view an old one or start a new draft from it.

\textbf{Path and who uses it:} “Form Builder” → “Test form” then “Publish” in the header, and “Versions” on the rail. Used by those who may edit the form.

\begin{rcnote}[Example: Horizon]
The system administrator tests “Branch visit request” as Omar Haddad, then as Sarah Al-Khatib, to make sure the “Approve” button appears for her alone. He then presses “Publish” and writes the reason for the change in the change note.

\section{Submissions}
\label{sec:builder-feature-forms-submissions}

Each time someone sends the form, a request is created, and the submissions page gathers the form's responses in one table. From it a reviewer opens a request to read its answers and see its status and approval steps, and a user starts a new request. The builder rail shows only the submission count; the table itself opens from the form in navigation.

\textbf{Path and who uses it:} navigation → the app → the section → the form's name. Used by those who fill the form and those who review its requests, each according to what “Access” gives them.
