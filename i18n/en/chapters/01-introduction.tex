\section{What is KayanOS?}
\label{sec:intro-minute}

Throughout this guide, we follow one fictional company from start to finish, connecting initial setup with its daily work.

\textbf{KayanOS} is one platform for running all of your organization's work: employees, tasks, projects, requests, correspondence and documents. Instead of scattered spreadsheets, lost messages and systems that do not talk to each other, everyone finds their work in one place, and managers can see who owns each piece of work and how far it has got.

KayanOS does not force a ready-made way of working on you. Alongside its ready apps, it gives you tools to build your own apps, forms and approval paths yourself, without writing any code. Every piece of information and every step stays governed by permissions that you set.

\section{Build your own system without code}
\label{sec:intro-no-code}

Every organization has work that is unlike anyone else's, such as a purchase request with its own approval stages, an equipment register or field-visit tracking. In many systems, this kind of work needs custom development. In KayanOS, an authorized administrator builds it in “Builder” by choosing and filling in settings, then publishes it for the team to use.

You start by deciding what one record represents, then add the tools your work needs. You can also describe what you need in your own words: the “AI Entity Builder” suggests fields and sections, and you review and adjust the suggestion before saving it, when this feature is enabled in your organization.

\section{Control every permission}
\label{sec:intro-control}

In KayanOS, people see only what they need for their work. Permissions are granted through roles: a role is a set of allowed “actions”, such as viewing, opening, adding, changing and deleting, which you give to the people who need it. You can set these permissions at several levels:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Apps and menus:} each member's navigation shows only what concerns their work.
  \item \textbf{Record types:} you decide who sees the records, and who adds, changes or deletes them.
  \item \textbf{Fields:} you hide a sensitive field, such as an amount, or stop unauthorized people from changing it.
  \item \textbf{Role rules:} you narrow a role by a value in the record, so each manager sees only their own branch's records.
  \item \textbf{Forms and approval paths:} you decide who fills in a request, who reviews it and who approves it.
\end{itemize}

Before relying on these settings, the “Check someone's access” tool shows what a specific person can actually see and do. So you know the effect of every change before it reaches the team.

\begin{rcnote}[Example: Horizon]
Omar Haddad can add and open a visit request, but he cannot change the approval decision; only Sarah Al-Khatib's role holds that action. The system administrator checks Omar's access before publishing the app.
\end{rcnote}
