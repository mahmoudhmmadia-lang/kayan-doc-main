\section{What is KayanOS, and why might you need it?}
\label{sec:intro-minute}
Imagine employee names kept in a file, work requests in chat threads, and due dates
in the manager's notebook. When a customer asks about a request, you have to ask
several people to find out who is handling it and where it stopped. As the team
grows, this searching becomes part of the daily work.

\textbf{\kos} is a workspace that brings together the management of employees, tasks,
projects, requests and files. It helps your team record work, name the person
responsible for it, follow its status, and go back to its information according
to each person's permissions.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{From a daily problem to clear use}
\label{tab:intro-minute}
\rcZebra
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{Y Y Y}
\rcHeaderRow
\thh{The problem at work} & \thh{What KayanOS offers} & \thh{The practical benefit}\\
We do not know who is handling the work or when it is due. & Tasks with an owner, a due date and a status. & Open the task to see who is working on it and what is left.\\
Employee and job data are scattered. & Members, positions and assignments in HR. & You know who holds each job and where they work.\\
Requests arrive in different ways and miss information. & Forms, requests and review flows set up to fit the work. & You collect the required information and follow the request in a known place.\\
Work documents are hard to find. & A files library and attachments linked to the work. & The team goes back to the document it needs.\\
The manager needs a picture of how work is going. & Statuses, projects and dashboards, once they are set up. & The manager reviews the recorded work and decides what needs follow-up.\\

\textbf{What your organization needs for success:} define responsibilities, enter
correct data, and agree on what “done” means. KayanOS lets you organize these things,
and the team keeps the information up to date while working so that follow-up is useful.

\section{A simple example: a working day at Horizon}
\label{sec:intro-entity}
Throughout this guide we use a fictional company called \textbf{Horizon Services Co.},
with a head office in Damascus and a branch in Aleppo. Sarah runs Human Resources,
and Omar is an Operations Coordinator at the Aleppo Branch.

A request arrives to prepare an office for a new employee. \textbf{Before the work is
organized}, the request may stay in a chat, and the manager asks more than once: who
is preparing the office? When will it be done? Where is the purchase invoice?

\textbf{With KayanOS}, the team can work like this, once the right tools are set up:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item The request and its information are recorded, so it has one place that all authorized people go back to.
  \item The manager sets out the work required from Omar, and its due date, in a clear task.
  \item Omar updates the task status and adds the result or the required document.
  \item The manager opens the work to review the result and follow what is not finished yet.
\end{enumerate}
This is an example of a way to organize work. Creating the request does not produce
the other steps automatically; any automatic move needs to be set up in advance to
fit your organization.

\section{First sign-in: where do you find your work?}
\label{sec:intro-app-map}
\textbf{Before you start:} ask your organization's administrator for its address and how
you sign in. The address looks like hzn.kayanos.app in our example; use your
organization's real address. If you are setting up the organization for the first
time, go to Chapter~\ref{ch:org-setup}.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Open the organization's address in your browser. Choose the sign-in method your
        administrator prepared for you: email, phone, or employee ID, then enter your sign-in details.
  \item After signing in, make sure you are in the right organization before entering any data.
  \item Open the “App launcher” from the home page, and choose “HR” for our exercise.
  \item Open “Workforce”, then “Members”. This is the list of employees, not the page of one employee.
  \item Open an employee's name to see their details. To go back, choose the page or app
        name in the navigation path at the top of the screen. You can also use search to find work or a person you have access to.
\end{enumerate}
