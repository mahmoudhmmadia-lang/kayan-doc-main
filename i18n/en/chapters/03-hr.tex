\section{What problem does this app help you solve?}
When employee, working-time and leave data are spread across different files, preparing
payroll starts with searching and matching. The HR app brings together a person's data,
job and working hours, and lets you review leaves and pay items in one flow.

At Horizon we will work through the example of one employee: we set their job and
working hours, review what was recorded, then check their pay before it is paid out.
Once the example works, we repeat it for the rest of the team.

\section{Who uses it, and when?}
The setup administrator prepares people and jobs the first time. The HR officer reviews
changes, working hours and leaves daily, and the person responsible for payroll reviews
pay periodically. An employee sees what their permissions allow, and does not need to
set up the salary scale or the organization's policies.

\section{Shifts: setting the expected working hours}
\label{sec:hr-shifts}
\textbf{A shift} sets the expected working days, hours and breaks. Create “Office Hours”
once for the team; review the actual check-in and check-out times in attendance.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{Choosing the shift type}
\label{tab:hr-shift-types}
\rcZebra
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{R{2.4cm} Y Y}
\rcHeaderRow
\thh{Type} & \thh{What do you enter?} & \thh{Example}\\
Regular & A set work period, with a break when needed. & From 09:00 to 17:00.\\
Split & More than one work period, without overlap. & From 09:00 to 13:00, then from 16:00 to 20:00.\\
Flexible & The required work duration in hours and minutes. & A duration of 08:00; no start and end period is set in the form.\\
Marathon & The required work duration, as the form shows it for this type. & The guide does not assume an automatic cycle of work and rest days from the name alone.\\
\end{tabularx}
\end{table}

\textbf{Try the Horizon example — done by the HR officer:}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Open “HR”, then “Time”, then “Shifts”, and choose to add.
        Type the name “Office Hours” and the code HZN-S-01, and choose the type “Regular”.
  \item Select Sunday, Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday and Thursday. Choose the time zone
        Asia/Damascus. The name, code, type and at least one working day are \req{}.
  \item In “Work Hours”, set the start to 09:00 and the end to 17:00.
        In “Breaks”, change the existing period to 13:00 to 13:30;
        do not add a second period for the example. The break is \opt{} and can be deleted when not needed.
  \item Check that “Total Hours” shows 07:30: eight hours minus half an hour.
        The system calculates this value. Click “Save”, then open the shift from the list and check the values.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Other types:} add the periods of a split shift separately. Enter the duration of a flexible or
marathon shift in hours and minutes. If saving is refused, correct the field that shows the message below it.

\begin{rcnote}[A shift that crosses midnight]
The period from 22:00 to 06:00 ends on the next day, and lasts eight hours
before breaks. Do not make the start time equal to the end time; that does not mean a full day.
Two periods must not overlap, and a break must not fall outside the work period.
\end{rcnote}

\textbf{When importing:} review the preview; the template asks for work hours and the duration,
while the manual entry fields differ by type.

\section{How does the shift reach the employee?}
\label{sec:hr-shift-inheritance}
The shift may come from the assignment itself, from the position, or from the organizational unit or the location.
“Inheritance” means using the shift set in a higher record instead of repeating it by hand.

Stop at the first shift found from the top. The system checks the unit and its parents before the location
and its parents; it does not combine schedules.

\begin{itemize}
  \item Put the shared schedule on the unit or the location, and check that it appears in the assignment.
  \item The “Override” option lets you choose a specific shift instead of the inherited one, when the field offers it.
        “Reset” goes back to using the inherited shift.
  \item When you change a team's schedule, review the assignments that have a specific shift; do not assume they
        will follow the change automatically.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Make Office Hours the schedule of Sarah's department:}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Open “HR”, then “Core”, then “Nodes”.
        Open “Human Resources” with the code HZN-HR.
  \item In the “Shift” field, choose “Office Hours”. If the field uses an inherited
        shift and does not accept a specific choice, use “Override”, then choose the shift and save.
  \item Open “Workforce”, then “Positions”, then “HR Manager”.
        Check that it is linked to the unit HZN-HR and that its shift is “Office Hours”.
  \item Open Sarah's assignment and check the shift. If a different shift appears, check the assignment and the position
        in the order shown in the figure; one of them may have a specific choice that takes priority over the department's shift.
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{Result:} Office Hours appears in Sarah's assignment. The example is a practice exercise you carry out yourself.

\section{Daily follow-up: what happened on the working day?}
\label{sec:hr-time}
Before explaining missing hours, review check-in and check-out, leave, holidays and corrections.
Deal with the cause of the difference before reviewing pay.

\textbf{Before you start:} you need an employee with an assignment and a shift, and attendance data recorded the way
your organization uses. Creating the employee and the shift does not add past attendance entries automatically.
\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{Tell setup, event and result apart}
\label{tab:hr-time}
\rcZebra
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{R{3.2cm} Y}
\rcHeaderRow
\thh{Page} & \thh{What do you do there?}\\
Timesheets & Choose the employees and the period to review the recorded working times.\\
Attendance & Choose the month, then the day to review the attendance details. Viewing another employee needs permission.\\
Holidays & Prepare the holiday group and its entries; a holiday can be a full day, part of a day, or a period.\\

Leaves & Record a leave event for an employee: the type, the date and the duration in the available fields.\\
\end{tabularx}

\textbf{Check one day — done by the employee or an authorized administrator:}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Open “HR”, then “Time”, then the attendance page. Choose the month you need.
        The employee reviews their own attendance; the administrator chooses the other member if the system allows it.
  \item Open a working day from the list. Compare the expected shift with the check-in and check-out times
        and the duration shown. Note what needs explaining, such as a missing check-out.
  \item Open “Time”, then “Timesheets” if you need to review entries over a period
        or for a group of employees. Set the employees and the date to match the period you are checking.
  \item If you find a shortfall, review the source of the entry with the person in charge of working hours. When there is a correction,
        open “Attendance Corrections” and check the employee, the date and the status before treating the problem as solved.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Check:} you can explain the difference between the expected and the recorded time. If the screen is empty,
check the employee, the period, the permission and the source of the entries; an empty screen alone does not prove an absence.

\begin{rcnote}[The shift and the attendance policy]
The shift sets the expected time; the attendance policy sets how lateness and overtime are handled.
Review the organization's policy and the result; do not assume a fixed deduction or automatic overtime pay.
\end{rcnote}

\section{Record the reason for an absence: holiday or leave?}
A holiday is a day or period the organization sets, while a leave belongs to one employee. Recording the right reason
helps the HR officer explain working hours and review their effect on pay.
\textbf{A leave type} is a recurring rule such as “Annual (paid)”, and \textbf{a leave record}
is an employee's use of that type on a specific date.

\textbf{Prepare the type once — HR officer:}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Open “HR”, then “Time”, then “Leave Types”. First search for
        the type the organization uses, to avoid creating another copy of it.
  \item If you are authorized to create a new type, choose to add, and type a clear name such as
        “Annual (paid)”. Choose the right payment method, and set the percentage if the type is partially paid.
  \item Save, then open the type from the list to check its name and payment method. Check that it is available
        to the employee in their job settings; the type existing does not on its own give everyone an entitlement.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Record Sarah's leave after it is approved under the organization's procedure:}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Open “Time”, then “Leaves”, and choose to add.
  \item Choose Sarah in “Member”, and the approved type in “Leave Type”.
        Enter the approved date and the duration in the matching day or hour fields.
        To try a full day, use one day on an agreed working date; do not record the same day
        once in days and again in hours.
  \item Complete the required fields in the organization's form and save. Open the leave record again
        and check the employee, the date, the type and the duration, then review the period when you review attendance and pay.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{For shared holidays:} open “Time”, then “Holiday Groups”, then the group
you need. Add the holiday with its name and date, and set a full day, part of a day, or a period.
For part of a day, enter the hours; for a period, enter its start and end. Save, then review the
group's details and its assignment to the departments or jobs concerned.

\textbf{Check:} you can tell Sarah's leave day apart from a holiday for the whole team. If the expected
effect does not appear, check the date, the type, its assignment and the review status; sending a leave request alone
does not prove the leave was approved and recorded.

\section{The salary scale and preparing pay}
\label{sec:hr-payroll}
The person responsible for payroll needs to explain the amount, not just a total.
The following flow lets you separate pay data from variable items, then calculate and review the employee's slip,
then gather the approved slips into a payroll. The authorized administrator carries it out after the working-hours review is complete.

\textbf{The salary scale} is a reference that links experience levels to seniority levels and pay values.
If the organization uses it, open the “Salary Scale” section: prepare the “Experience Levels” group
with its levels, raise rule and currency, then the “Seniority Levels” and the pay values linked to them.
Save each setting, then open the “Salary Scale” page and review the values where a seniority level meets an experience level.
A scale alone is not enough to prepare an employee's pay; also review their assignment data, pay and currency.

\textbf{Prepare the calculation data:}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Open the employee's assignment and check the work period, the pay and the currency, and review the working hours,
        leaves and corrections for the period you will calculate.
  \item Open “HR”, then “Payment”, then “Payroll Inputs”. Search for
        an existing input before adding a new bonus or deduction for the same period.
  \item When adding, choose the employee, the date, the input type, its direction and the calculation method,
        and enter the approved value. Save, then open the input and use “Preview calculation”
        to review the amount. Complete the input's approval according to your permissions and the organization's procedure.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Result:} the pay data and variable items are ready for review. If the preview differs from what
you expected, correct the value or the calculation method before moving on to the slip; a preview does not mean payment.

\section{From the employee's slip to the payroll}
\textbf{Example flow:} review Sarah's slip first, then add it to the payroll for the required period.
Use the pay and currency your organization has approved; the guide's examples do not set real pay.

\textbf{Create and review the slip:}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Open “HR”, then “Payment”, then “Salary Slips”, and click
        “Create salary slips”.
  \item Choose “Period end”. The eligible rows appear, and each row links a member to a position.
        Select Sarah's row and her position. Check “Period start”; on the first slip it can be changed,
        and the assignment start date is used by default.
  \item Click the button that creates the drafts and shows the selected count, then open Sarah's slip.
        If the system stops you because a draft already exists, open that draft and review it first.
  \item Click “Calculate”. Review the period, the earning and deduction items and the amount due.
        If the inputs changed, use “Recalculate” before approving.
  \item Once the result matches the approved documents, click “Approve” according to your permission,
        read the confirmation message, then confirm the approval. Go back to the list and check that the approved
        status appears. Do not approve a slip whose items have not been explained.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Gather the slips into a payroll:}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Open “Payment”, then “Payrolls”, and choose “Generate payroll”.
  \item Set the month and the year, review the filters, then choose the employees you need.
        The payroll is based on the approved slips within the selected range.
  \item Click “Generate”, and review any conflict message with an existing payroll before going on.
        Open the resulting payroll and check the employees, the period, the total and the status.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Check:} you can trace the amount in the payroll back to the employee's slip and its items.
If an employee does not appear, check the period, the filters, the assignment and the slip status before creating new data.

\begin{rcwarn}[Calculation is not payment]
Creating pay or a payroll does not prove the money reached the employee. Review the payment status and the payment records
according to the organization's procedure. Do not treat the practice example's figures as real pay.
\end{rcwarn}

\section{How it connects to other apps}
Tasks and projects use the people and jobs you set up here. The organization may also use
requests to ask for or approve a leave; follow its approved flow, and do not treat sending
a request as a completed leave record. When reviewing a project's cost, go back to the available payroll
distribution details instead of assuming that each salary belongs to a single project.

Once people and working hours are stable, the next step is following up work in the \textbf{Tasks} app.
