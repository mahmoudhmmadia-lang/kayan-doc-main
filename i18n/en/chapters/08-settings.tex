\chapter{Settings}
\label{ch:settings}

\section{What you will achieve}

The Settings app holds the decisions that apply to the whole organization: its identity, who can get in, what applicants are told, and the channels it uses. These pages usually belong to the system administrator; employees do not need them for daily work. Some pages appear only to people allowed to manage them.

\begin{rcnote}[Example: Horizon]
Horizon’s system administrator reviews the organization settings every three months. When Sarah Al-Khatib asks for two-step verification, the administrator opens the page under “Access and security”, changes the setting, and confirms its effect before telling the team.
\end{rcnote}

\section{Settings page directory}
\label{sec:settings-pages}
The pages follow the eight groups on the Settings page, in the same order. Start with “Organization” and “Access and security”, then move to the groups your organization uses. If you have no public portal, for example, skip its pages.
