\section{Capacity and permissions}
\label{sec:correspondence-feature-capacity}

Your capacity is the position in whose name you handle correspondence. Your account may have several positions, and a valid delegation may make another position available. Switching capacity does not grant new permission; available actions still depend on the letter, assignment, delegation and permissions.

\section{Addressees and the approval chain}
\label{sec:correspondence-feature-recipients}

“Addressees” sets where the letter goes, while “Approval chain” sets who reviews, approves or signs it before it is issued. The two serve different purposes, so do not add an approver to “To” just because they will agree to the letter.

\begin{rcnote}[Example: Horizon]
Omar adds Aleppo Branch at ① and leaves ② and ③ unchanged so the letter passes through the registry. He then reads the route at ④ and adds the General Manager at ⑤ to sign the letter.
\end{rcnote}
\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.95\linewidth,height=0.72\textheight,keepaspectratio]{figures/screens/ch07-recipients-en.png}
\caption{Addressees and approval chain on a test draft}
\label{fig:correspondence-recipients-screen}
\shotnote{The image shows a test draft with one addressee and two manually added stages. Together, the two panels show where the letter goes and who agrees to it, so review both before sending. ① “To”: search for a body, position or group, or type an external body’s name. ② “Copy for information”. ③ “Send directly without the registry”: for one internal position only. ④ “Expected routing”: the letter’s way to the addressee, not its approval stages. ⑤ The “Approval chain” stages: only the last stage signs.}

\begin{enumerate}
\item Type part of the name in “To” and choose the right result from the list. A position shows its unit and holders to help you tell similar names apart.

\item Read “Expected routing”. Turn on direct sending only when the letter goes to one internal position.

\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Check and recover:} Each addressee appears in its field, and “Approval chain” holds the required stages after saving. If the chain stays empty, do not rely on the “Send for approval” button label: a letter with no stages is issued when sent. Add the required stage or ask the administrator before sending.

\textbf{Practice and check:} Run your role’s row in the table, then write down three things: the status the letter reached, who owns the next step, and the evidence you saw on screen. If the path stops, check your capacity, then your permission, then the type’s settings before repeating the action. You have mastered the chapter when you can state these three things without looking at the example.
